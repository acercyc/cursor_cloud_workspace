# Guiding question

*To what extent do perturbational complexity measures (PCI, PCIst, related signal-diversity measures) index proximity to criticality in cortical dynamics — and is this relationship necessary, contingent, or coincidental for consciousness?*

# Ground rules for the agent

- **Audience:** a single researcher learning the topic — optimize for clarity and synthesis, not exhaustiveness.
- **Cite everything** with author, year, journal, and a link (DOI or publisher page). Prefer primary sources; verify citations actually exist — do not fabricate.
- **Timebox:** deep-read only the ~10–15 most central papers; skim the rest (abstract, figures, discussion).
- **Flag uncertainty** explicitly: distinguish *established findings*, *modeling results*, and *speculative claims*.
- Keep a running reading log (see Deliverables).

# Phase 1 — Foundations (two parallel tracks)

## Track A: Brain near-criticality

- [ ]  Read and summarize a modern review (e.g., O'Byrne & Jerbi 2022, *Trends in Neurosciences*, "How critical is brain criticality?"; Zimmern 2020, *Front. Neural Circuits*).
- [ ]  Cover the classics: Beggs & Plenz 2003 (neuronal avalanches); Kinouchi & Copelli 2006 (dynamic range); Wilting & Priesemann 2018 (branching ratio, reverberating regime, subsampling problem).
- [ ]  Produce a short explainer distinguishing the **non-equivalent notions of criticality**: avalanche/absorbing-state criticality, edge of chaos, edge of synchronization — and which empirical markers belong to which (avalanche exponents, branching ratio m, long-range temporal correlations, chaoticity estimates).
- [ ]  Summarize the case for **near** (slightly subcritical) rather than exactly critical operation: safety margin, tunability, state dependence (attention, arousal, anesthesia).

## Track B: PCI and perturbational complexity

- [ ]  Read and summarize Casali et al. 2013 (*Sci Transl Med*) — original PCI: TMS–EEG method, binarization, Lempel–Ziv compression, normalization.
- [ ]  Casarotto et al. 2016 — clinical validation and the PCI* ≈ 0.31 cutoff in disorders of consciousness.
- [ ]  Comolatti et al. 2019 — PCIst simplified variant.
- [ ]  Note the empirical track record across states: wake, REM, NREM, propofol/xenon/midazolam, ketamine, psychedelics, DoC patients (including covert consciousness cases).
- [ ]  Explain the rationale: integration + differentiation, IIT background, why direct cortical perturbation instead of sensory stimulation.

# Phase 2 — The bridge (core of the review)

- [ ]  **Mechanism:** Pigorini et al. 2015 (*NeuroImage*) and Rosanova et al. 2018 (*Nat Commun*) — cortical bistability and OFF-periods as the cause of low PCI; reinterpret through a criticality lens (departure from the asynchronous near-critical regime).
- [ ]  **Dynamics/stability:** Solovey et al. 2015 (*J Neurosci*) — anesthesia shifts cortical dynamics from near-instability (criticality) toward stability.
- [ ]  **Most direct link:** Toker et al. 2022 (*PNAS*), "Consciousness is supported by near-critical slow cortical electrodynamics" — edge-of-chaos proximity vs. information-richness across wake, anesthesia, seizure, psychedelics.
- [ ]  **Modeling:** find computational studies that tune a network's distance from criticality and compute simulated PCI or perturbational responses (whole-brain models, spiking networks; e.g., work in the Deco/Kringelbach tradition, Kim & Lee ~2019–2020, and related).
- [ ]  **Search strategy:** intersect forward citations of Casali 2013 and Toker 2022 filtered by keywords *critical / criticality / bistability / edge of chaos*; also search combinations of {criticality, avalanches, branching ratio, edge of chaos, bistability} × {perturbational complexity, PCI, PCIst, TMS-EEG, Lempel-Ziv, signal diversity} × {consciousness, anesthesia, sleep, disorders of consciousness}. Include recent work (2022–present) since the field is moving fast.

# Phase 3 — Tensions and dissociations

- [ ]  Psychedelics and ketamine: complexity at or above waking levels — does this fit or break the "near-critical = conscious" picture?
- [ ]  Which transition does PCI actually track? Do different criticality notions make different predictions?
- [ ]  Methodological confounds: power laws without criticality, subsampling biases in branching-ratio estimates, arousal vs. consciousness, PCI's dependence on stimulation site and preprocessing.

# Phase 4 — Synthesis deliverables

1. **Synthesis essay (2–4 pages):** a defended answer to the guiding question — is PCI an operational measure of distance from criticality: yes, no, or partly?
2. **Concept map / quadrant figure:** states (wake, REM, NREM, propofol, ketamine, psychedelics, seizure, DoC subtypes) plotted on *distance-from-criticality* vs. *PCI/complexity* axes; highlight dissociations from the diagonal.
3. **Evidence table** with columns: Study | Type (empirical / model / review) | System & state | Criticality measure | Complexity measure | Direction of relationship | Which transition assumed | Key caveat.
4. **Three-bucket summary:** what's established / what's assumed / what dissociates.
5. **Open questions list:** e.g., no study has causally tuned criticality while measuring PCI in the same system; candidate experiments.
